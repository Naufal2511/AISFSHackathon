1Approach

Measurement first, judgement second. Deterministic code detects, tracks and places every cow on the pen floor in metres. Agents check that record with tools, and code checks the agents. Every output says whether it was observed, agent-verified, estimated or not visible.

Seeing the cows

COCO YOLO missed black Angus at the back fence and at night. We hand-labelled 216 frames (split by clip, day and IR) and fine-tuned YOLO26-m at 1280 px on HPRC. ByteTrack follows each cow within one camera only; who is who is never left to the tracker. Per-camera homographies from pen-map landmarks put every box on one floor map in metres.

Knowing who is who

Black Angus look alike to appearance models: two different cows score 0.75–0.94 DINOv2 similarity. Identity therefore rests on geometry and time, and appearance only breaks ties.

  • Agreement. Tracks in two cameras that stand on the same spot for the same seconds must be one cow. Tracks seen together but far apart cannot be.
  • Consensus. Each camera’s belief over the 10 cows is pulled towards its neighbours’ beliefs. A Lyapunov energy that never rises keeps IDs from flickering.
  • Anchors. These are seconds when every cow is ≥ 2.5 m from the rest, fully visible, held by one stable track and placed within 2 m by every camera. There the identity set is fixed.
  • Memory cards. Each card holds a cow's clearest crops by day and IR. A person confirms the cards once, and the agent keeps them current.

Choosing the models

On 8 identical identity cases GPT-5.6 Luna took 12.8 s each and always passed its self-check first time (Sonnet 5: 31.8 s; Gemini 3.5 Flash: 88 % within budget; local Qwen2.5-VL: 75 % first-time pass). Luna runs the identity loop; Opus the risk cases.

Reading behavior

We built ~330 Label Studio tasks (one cow, one camera, 5 s) over all cameras, both days and nights. A DINOv2-S classifier learned from 181 of them plus 9,269 public CBVD-5 crops. Its output joins geometric cues: box height against standing height, floor speed and zone dwell. Rumination is called only when chewing is measured: a jaw-motion spectrum must show 55–75 movements/min. Cameras are fused as experts (the bunk camera is trusted for feeding, not posture); a sticky HMM with bout-length priors removes impossible jumps.

Agents that check, and are checked

Every agent runs one loop: a trigger picks cases worth a look and a playbook suggests tool calls. The model answers in JSON that must cite evidence it gathered. Code then checks the answer, allows one revision, and otherwise downgrades it to uncertain, so nothing becomes a silent guess.

Why stitching48 h of 4K from three cameras did not fit one machine in time, so 33 chunks ran in parallel: Day 1 on three laptops, Day 2 as an HPRC GPU array job. Each chunk numbers its cows on its own, so every chunk also tracks the 2 min before its window. The stitcher joins IDs where two chunks saw the same boxes in the same frames (IoU ≥ 0.5, Hungarian). Of 290 joins, 204 came from that overlap and 13 from a clear appearance margin. The other 73 are weak and listed in id_map.csv for review.

Level 2 · BRD risk ranking

We score 13 indicators from the BRD literature: bunk time and visits, how slowly a cow joins the herd's feed rush (the DART "slow to come to eat" sign), distance walked, lying time and bout length, distance from herdmates, time alone or out of step, rumination, drinking, grooming, resting breath rate and sudden movements. Each is a robust z against the same pen at the same hours, so weather and feeding times cancel out. Only the sick direction counts fully, short coverage shrinks the score, and the change from day 1 to day 2 counts at half weight. A typical cow scores about 0.10. A visible sign changes the score only after its evidence passes the self-check.

3Observed vs estimated · limits

  • Observed means a detection we can point to (camera, second, box). Estimated is carried or filled and labelled so. Risk uses observed time only.
  • 30 % of cow-minutes are not visible, mostly occlusion in the resting area and far corners at night. Time budgets give shares of visible time too.
  • Identity across chunks is the weakest link. 25 % of joins are low-margin. A wrong join blurs differences between cows more than it invents them.
  • Rumination is over-called. Much lying is labelled ruminating, so it carries the lowest behavior weight (0.3).
  • Ten cows and 48 h cannot validate a BRD model. With no diagnoses, the score is a ranked suspicion index, not a probability. Clinical-sign diagnosis is itself only ~62 % sensitive and 63 % specific (White & Renter 2009).
  • Discharge needs close daylight colour, which overhead and IR views rarely give, so most sign checks end as not assessable.

4With more data

  • Outcome labels. Temperature, DART scores, lung ultrasound or treatment records would let us fit and calibrate the weights instead of taking them from the literature.
  • Longer baselines. One to two weeks would let each cow act as her own control (CUSUM on feeding and activity). That is where the 3–6 day lead before diagnosis appears in the literature.
  • Identity without stitching. One continuous run, or RFID at the bunk, would remove the weak joins. A cattle keypoint model would measure head carriage and ears directly.
  • Harder classes. 50+ clips each of rumination, grooming and social interaction, a video model, and a head-height bunk camera for discharge and ear droop.
  • Live early warning (Level 3). The pipeline already runs in 1 h resumable chunks. The risk agent's clips, evidence images and action line are what a pen rider's alert needs.
